# FlashPDF CSS compatibility

FlashPDF renders a static CSS subset. It does not embed a browser engine, fetch assets, execute JavaScript in CSS, or support container queries, Grid, positioned layout, transforms, filters, animations, or SVG layout beyond the supported inline drawing subset. `@media` supports `print`, `screen`, and width conditions only.

The public authoring API is native JSX plus `render`. `PageNumber` and `TotalPages` work only in a repeating `RenderOptions.header` or `RenderOptions.footer`, and the characters they stand for, U+001E and U+001F, reject in your own text. There are no `Document`, `Text`, `Stack`, `Row`, preview, or download helpers; tables use the HTML `table` tags.

## Authoring

FlashPDF supports a small static JSX vocabulary, not browser HTML compatibility. Use `main`, `div`, `section`, `article`, `header`, `footer`, `nav`, `aside`, `p`, headings, `pre`, `blockquote`, `address`, `figure`, `figcaption`, `ul`, `ol`, `li`, `dl`, `dt`, `dd`, `span`, `b`, `strong`, `em`, `i`, `cite`, `small`, `sub`, `sup`, `u`, `ins`, `s`, `del`, `mark`, `code`, `kbd`, `samp`, `a`, `br`, `hr`, `img`, and `svg`. A paragraph is a sequence of inline runs: adjacent text and the inline elements (`span` through `samp`, and `a`) flow together and wrap as one, each run keeping its own font, weight, style, size, and colour, so `<p>Hello <b>World</b></p>` is one line. `em`, `i`, and `cite` are italic by default, as in browsers. A mixed line takes the tallest run's height. `br` forces a line break; `br` and `hr` take no children, and `br` takes no box or layout styles. Whitespace collapses to one space across runs and is trimmed at line ends. Whitespace-only text between block-level children or flex items, such as Prettier's `{" "}`, renders nothing, as in CSS. An inline element with margin, padding, border, background, `width`, `flex`, or its own `text-align` remains a standalone layout node; `mark` is the exception for its background, below. Paragraphs and unpainted boxes split across pages line by line, and a word wider than its column breaks at the last glyph that fits. Flex rows, `break-inside: avoid` groups, and painted, padded, or bordered boxes are atomic: they move whole to the next page and reject only when taller than an empty page.

Tables use `table`, `thead`, `tbody`, `tfoot`, `tr`, `th`, and `td`. Column widths come from the first row's cells: `width` in `pt`, `px`, `rem`, or `%`, or `flex`, exactly as flex-row children; cells without either share the remainder equally. Every row must have that many cells, and `colSpan`/`rowSpan` reject, as does a `td` or `th` outside a table row. A cell holds text, inline runs, or nested blocks; a row is as tall as its tallest cell and every cell stretches to it, so cell backgrounds and borders tile the row. A table splits between rows: `thead` rows repaint at the top of every page it continues on, `tfoot` rows repaint after the last body row of every page, and a row that does not fit moves whole to the next page under the repeated header. `th` is bold and centred by default. A `td`/`th` accepts padding, border, background, and text properties; a `tr` accepts a background that paints under its cells; a `table` accepts `width`, vertical margins, and `break-inside: avoid`. Text wraps inside a narrow cell rather than clipping.

```tsx
<table style={{ width: "100%" }}>
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Item</th>
      <th style={{ width: "80pt", textAlign: "right" }}>Amount</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    {items.map((item) => (
      <tr key={item.id}>
        <td>{item.name}</td>
        <td style={{ textAlign: "right" }}>{item.total}</td>
      </tr>
    ))}
  </tbody>
</table>
```

Lists use `ul`, `ol`, and `li`. A `ul` or `ol` takes only `li` children, and an `li` belongs in one. Browser defaults apply: 40px of left padding, `1em` vertical margins that drop to zero in a nested list, and `list-style-type` `disc` for a `ul`, then `circle` and `square` one and two lists deeper, and `decimal` for an `ol`. `list-style-type` takes `disc`, `circle`, `square`, `decimal`, `decimal-leading-zero`, `lower-alpha`, `lower-latin`, `upper-alpha`, `upper-latin`, `lower-roman`, `upper-roman`, and `none`. `list-style-position` takes `outside`, the default, or `inside`. The `list-style` shorthand sets both and, as in CSS, resets a part it omits, so `list-style: inside` on an `ol` brings back `disc`. `ol` takes `start` and `reversed`, and `li` takes `value` in an `ol` only. A number outside its counter's range, such as zero in `lower-alpha` or 4000 in `lower-roman`, falls back to decimal. The marker belongs to the first line of the item's first paragraph and moves with it across pages. An outside marker hangs left of the item's text, right-aligned against it, in the item's font, size, and colour; an inside marker starts the first line. Bullets are shapes the engine draws, not glyphs, so they need no glyph in the font and work under PDF/A; counters are text, so their digits and letters must exist in the font. An item that begins with a nested list, table, or image puts its marker on a line of its own. `list-style-image`, string markers, and other counter styles reject. In tagged mode a list item is a paragraph and its counter is a second one: there is no `L`, `LI`, `Lbl`, or `LBody` structure yet.

`dl`, `dt`, `dd`, `blockquote`, `address`, `figure`, `figcaption`, `nav`, and `aside` are blocks with the browser defaults: `blockquote` and `figure` have `1em 40px` margins, `dl` has `1em` vertical margins, `dd` indents 40px, and `address` is italic. Vertical margins collapse, so two `blockquote`s in a row are `1em` apart, as in a browser.

`pre` is a monospace block with `1em` vertical margins and `white-space: pre`. `white-space` takes `normal`, `nowrap`, `pre`, `pre-wrap`, and `pre-line`, and anything else rejects. `pre` and `pre-wrap` keep spaces and newlines, `pre-line` keeps newlines and collapses spaces, and `nowrap` collapses whitespace and never wraps. A tab moves to the next multiple of 8 characters from the line start; the count is in characters, so a proportional font only approximates it. A `pre` or `nowrap` line wider than its container rejects with `LineTooWide` and the element path, where `pre-wrap` wraps it. One newline right after `<pre>` is dropped, as in HTML, so a template literal can start on its own line. The engine collapses ASCII spaces, so `pre` and `pre-wrap` keep them as U+00A0: a font without that glyph shows its space glyph, and copied text has no-break spaces. A paragraph record holds 64 KiB, which a `pre` of about 800 average lines fills; split a longer listing into several `pre` blocks with `margin: 0`.

`small`, `sub`, and `sup` are `0.83em`. `sub` and `sup` shift the baseline down by a fifth or up by a third of the parent's font size, and nested ones add up. `vertical-align` takes `baseline`, `sub`, and `super` on inline elements; it rejects on blocks and table cells, on an inline element that has box styles, and with any other value. `u` and `ins` underline, `s` and `del` strike through, `code`, `kbd`, and `samp` use the `monospace` family, and `mark` is black on yellow. A `mark` background paints behind its text, one rectangle per line segment covering the font's ascent and descent; it is the only inline element whose background does not make a layout node.


Images use `img` with `src` as the PNG, JPEG, or SVG file bytes in a `Uint8Array`, passed by the host exactly like fonts: FlashPDF fetches nothing, and a string path or `data:` URL rejects with a hint. In tagged mode, nonempty `alt` becomes the figure description and empty `alt` marks a decorative image. An image is an atomic block box, left-aligned in its container. `width` and `height` take `pt`, `px`, or (width only) `%` of the container width; given one, the other follows the image's aspect ratio; given neither, the pixel size at 96 px per inch applies, capped to the container width. Images work as block children, in table cells, in flex rows, and in decorated boxes, and a paragraph splits around them at page ends, but an image cannot sit inside a paragraph's text. The same `Uint8Array` used on every row embeds once.

Vector drawings use `svg` and lay out exactly like `img`: the `width` and `height` attributes are the intrinsic size in CSS pixels, style `width` and `height` resize the box, and the drawing is clipped to the box. Without `width` and `height`, the `viewBox` size in pixels applies; without a `viewBox`, the drawing scales as if it were `0 0 width height`. `viewBox` and `preserveAspectRatio` (`none`, or `xMinYMin` to `xMaxYMax` with `meet` or `slice`) map the drawing into the box. Inside go `g`, `rect` with `rx`/`ry`, `circle`, `ellipse`, `line`, `polyline`, `polygon`, and `path` with the full `d` grammar (relative commands, `H`/`V`, `S`/`T`, `Q`, and arcs, which become cubic curves), plus `title` and `desc`. Every element takes `transform` (`matrix`, `translate`, `scale`, `rotate`, `skewX`, `skewY`) and the presentation attributes `fill`, `fill-rule`, `fill-opacity`, `stroke`, `stroke-width`, `stroke-linecap`, `stroke-linejoin`, `stroke-miterlimit`, `stroke-dasharray`, `stroke-dashoffset`, `stroke-opacity`, and `opacity`, under their React names (`strokeWidth`) or SVG names. A `style` object or declaration string on a shape or `g` takes the same properties and wins over the attributes. Colours take the CSS colour syntax plus `none` and `currentColor`, the element's CSS `color`. In tagged mode, `aria-label` or the first `<title>` becomes the figure description, and a drawing with neither is an artifact. The same drawing on every row embeds once. `QRCode` and `Barcode` return an `svg`, so they size, clip, and tag the same way. `<img src={svgBytes}>` takes an SVG file with the same subset: FlashPDF parses the markup itself and needs no DOM.

```tsx
<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" width={32} aria-label="Paid" style={{ color: '#0a7' }}>
  <circle cx={12} cy={12} r={10} fill="none" stroke="currentColor" strokeWidth={2} />
  <path d="M7 12l3 3 7-7" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" strokeLinecap="round" />
</svg>
```

Links use `a` with an absolute `http:`, `https:`, or `mailto:` `href`, or `#id` to jump to the element with that `id`. Inside a paragraph an `a` is an inline run like `b`; around an `img` or a block it makes that content clickable. Every wrapped line of a link gets its own rectangle. `a` defaults to `color: #0000EE` and `text-decoration: underline`, and a rule or inline style overrides both.

```tsx
<a href="https://example.com/invoices/42">
  <img src={logoBytes} alt="Acme Supply Co." style={{ width: 120 }} />
</a>
<p>
  Questions? <a href="mailto:billing@example.com">Email billing</a>
</p>
```

A link to `#id` is a `GoTo` action to a destination at the top of the target's first line, at the left edge of its content box, with the viewer's zoom unchanged. The destination sits on the page where that line lands, so a target that moves to the next page takes its destination with it. Every target is also a named destination, so `invoice.pdf#nameddest=terms` opens at `<section id="terms">` in a viewer. The id is matched exactly: printable ASCII, 1 to 255 characters, no percent-decoding. An element can be a target when it is a block (`div`, `main`, `section`, `article`, `header`, `footer`), `p`, a heading, `img`, `hr`, `table`, `td`, `th`, or a block-level `span` or `a`. An id that a link names must be on exactly one element. Ids that no link names stay free for CSS selectors and may repeat. A link to a missing or repeated id, or to an id on an inline `span`, `b`, `strong`, or `a` inside a paragraph, a `br`, a `tr`, or a table row group, rejects naming the id.

`RenderOptions.outline` bookmarks every `h1` to `h6` in the body, in document order. A heading nests under the nearest earlier heading with a lower level, so an `h3` after an `h1` sits directly under it. The title is the heading's text with whitespace collapsed, cut to 255 characters; a heading with no text gets no bookmark. `data-bookmark="Title"` on a heading replaces the title, and `data-bookmark={false}` leaves the heading out. Headings in a header or footer never get one. A PDF with bookmarks opens with the outline panel showing. Link targets and bookmarks share a limit of 10,000 per render.

```tsx
<h2 id="terms" data-bookmark="Terms of payment">Terms</h2>
<p>See the <a href="#terms">terms</a>.</p>
```

Compile CSS in the consuming app's build step, then pass the resulting string to `render`. CSS Modules and minification belong to that build step. `stylesheet` validates the final CSS against FlashPDF's supported subset.

```ts
import { render } from '@pettersen3008/flashpdf';

const css = '.invoice { padding: 18pt; background: #fff }';
const pdf = await render(<main className="invoice">Invoice</main>, {
  stylesheets: [css],
});
```

The CSS may come from CSS Modules or any tool that emits static CSS. Runnable build-step examples cover [Tailwind](./packages/flashpdf/examples/css-integrations/tailwind.mjs), [StyleX](./packages/flashpdf/examples/css-integrations/stylex.mjs), and [styled-components](./packages/flashpdf/examples/css-integrations/styled-components.mjs). Run all three with `pnpm --filter @pettersen3008/flashpdf examples:css`.

Tailwind's example imports `tailwindcss/theme.css` with `theme(inline)`, so colour and spacing utilities carry their values instead of `var()` references to a `:root` rule in `@layer theme`, which FlashPDF skips, and `tailwindcss/utilities.css` with `source(none)`, so it builds only the listed utilities, unlayered. It renders an invoice from that build; the Tailwind v4 section of the [guide](./docs/guide.md#tailwind-v4) lists the utilities that render and the ones that reject. StyleX passes Babel metadata through `processStylexRules`. styled-components' server output also contains hydration metadata with unsupported attribute selectors and `content`; its example selects the generated component rule before calling `render`. FlashPDF adds no runtime adapter for any of them.

Unsupported declarations fail at `render` only for rules that match an element, naming the property, selector, and source position. `stylesheet(source)` validates the properties and values of every rule with a supported selector up front. Custom property definitions and values that use `var()`, `calc()`, `min()`, `max()`, `clamp()`, or `color-mix()` are checked by `render` where a rule applies, since only the cascade resolves them and Tailwind puts them in most utilities. Property names are case-insensitive, and a camelCase name such as `fontSize` rejects with its kebab-case spelling:

```
unsupported CSS property: border-radius (box corners are square) in selector ".total" at 4:3
```

At-rules other than `@media` and `@property` are skipped. So is a rule whose selector can match nothing in a PDF, such as `:hover`, `::before`, or `button`. A selector that could match an element but is outside the subset, such as `[href]` or `:has()`, rejects at `stylesheet()` and at `render`, naming the selector and position, whether or not any element matches:

```
unsupported CSS selector: :has() (not implemented) in selector ".card:has(.badge)" at 2:1
```

Every runtime error names the element and its ancestors, for example `on <span.total> in <div.row> > <main.invoice>`. FlashPDF never drops a declaration that applies to an element it renders.

## Supported subset

- Selectors: tags (case-insensitive, as in HTML), `*`, `.class`, `#id`, and compounds of them, joined by the descendant, child `>`, next-sibling `+`, and subsequent-sibling `~` combinators. Class and id names may use CSS escapes, so `.w-1\/2`, `.w-\[120px\]`, and `.\32xl\:p-4` match the classes `w-1/2`, `w-[120px]`, and `2xl:p-4`.
- Pseudo-classes: `:root`, `:empty`, `:first-child`, `:last-child`, `:only-child`, `:nth-child()`, `:nth-last-child()`, the same five with `-of-type`, and `:is()`, `:where()`, and `:not()` with a selector list, complex selectors included, nested up to 16 levels. `:nth-*()` takes `An+B`, `odd`, or `even`; `of <selector>` rejects. These are what Tailwind's `first:`, `last:`, `only:`, `odd:`, `even:`, `empty:`, `space-y-*`, and `divide-y` compile to.
- Element siblings are counted on the resolved tree: components, fragments, arrays, `null`, and `false` have flattened away, and text does not count. The siblings of an element are the other element children of its parent, so in `<div>Total <p /> <p /></div>` the first `<p>` is `:first-child` and the second is `:nth-child(2)`. `:empty` matches an element whose children are all zero-length text, so a single space makes it non-empty. The top-level elements of the render input are siblings of each other, each one is `:root`, and the `header` and `footer` options are each a root of their own.
- Specificity as an (ids, classes, tags) tuple, then source order across the whole `stylesheets` array, so a later sheet wins a tie. `:is()` and `:not()` count their most specific argument, `:where()` counts nothing, and every other pseudo-class counts as a class. Inline styles beat both, and `!important` declarations beat all non-important ones. A shorthand and its longhands cascade in that order too, so a later `border: none` clears an earlier `border-bottom`, and a later `background` replaces `background-color`. An inline value of `undefined` is skipped, as React does.
- Matching cost is polynomial in the selector and the tree. Each descendant, subsequent-sibling, and nested-selector step caches one verdict per element, so a long selector on a deep or wide tree does not backtrack.
- `calc()`, `min()`, `max()`, and `clamp()` over lengths and plain numbers, evaluated after `var()` substitution with `+ - * /`, CSS precedence, and parentheses. `+`, `-`, `min()`, `max()`, and `clamp()` take operands of one kind (number, length, or percentage), `*` needs a number on one side, and `/` a nonzero number on the right. `em` resolves against the element's font size, and against the parent's inside `font-size`. A percentage result works for `width`, `height`, and `line-height`, and only when the whole expression is a percentage: `calc(1 / 2 * 100%)` is `50%`, but `calc(100% - 4pt)` rejects because the engine resolves widths later. An expression holds at most 1024 characters and 16 nested levels, and only a call at the top of a value is evaluated, so `rgb(calc(...))` rejects.
- Logical properties for horizontal left-to-right text: `margin-inline`, `margin-block`, `padding-inline`, and `padding-block` take one value, or two as start then end, and each has `-start` and `-end` longhands. Inline start is left, inline end is right, block start is top, and block end is bottom, and a later physical longhand overrides them in cascade order. Right-to-left and vertical writing modes do not exist, and `border-inline*` and `border-block*` (Tailwind's `border-x` and `border-y`) reject.
- `border-style` and the per-side `border-top-style`, `border-right-style`, `border-bottom-style`, and `border-left-style` take `solid`, `none`, or `hidden`. `none` and `hidden` clear the width whatever the declaration order, and `dotted`, `dashed`, `double`, `groove`, `ridge`, `inset`, and `outset` reject. The per-side `border-*-width` and `border-*-color` work too. A width with no style draws solid, unlike a browser, where the style defaults to `none`, and a style with no width draws nothing.
- `@media` with `print` or `all` applies and `screen` is skipped. `(width >= 48rem)`, `(width < 48rem)`, `(min-width: 30rem)`, and `(max-width: 30rem)` compare the page content width, which is the page width minus both margins, in points; `rem` is 12 pt. They combine with `and`, `,`, `not`, and `only`, and `@media` blocks nest 16 levels deep. With A4 and the default 36 pt margin the content is 523 pt, so Tailwind's `sm:` (480 pt) applies and `md:` (576 pt) does not until the margin is 9.5 pt or less. Any other query, such as `(prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, rejects with its reason where a rule inside it matches an element, and `stylesheet()` accepts it.
- `@property` registers a custom property: `initial-value` is the value of an element that sets none, ahead of a `var()` fallback, and `inherits: false` stops inheritance. `syntax` is required but not checked. Tailwind's `--tw-border-style: solid` default works this way, so its `border` utility draws. The `@layer properties { @supports ... }` fallback for browsers without `@property` is skipped with every other `@layer` and `@supports` block.
- Inherited text properties and `var(--name, fallback)`. The fallback may hold functions and a nested `var()`, such as `var(--c, rgb(0, 0, 0))` or `var(--a, var(--b))`.
- `inherit` and `initial` on every supported property. `inherit` takes the parent's value, and an `em` length the parent declared resolves again against this element's font size. `currentcolor` is the element's own `color`.
- `pt`, `px`, `em`, `rem`, unitless values where accepted, and percent widths in flex rows. `width` and image `height` take `pt`, `px`, `rem`, or (width only) `%`.
- Block flow, flex rows/columns, `gap` between flex row items and between column children, page size, and document margins.
- `line-height` as `normal` (for the built-in fonts, the browser's: Helvetica and Times are 1.15em and Courier is 1.133em, the hhea ascent, descent, and line gap of Arial, Times New Roman, and Courier New, with the line gap split above and below the text; an embedded font uses its own metrics), a unitless factor, a percent, or a length. A factor inherits as a factor; a percent or `em` resolves against the element's own font size and inherits as a length. The extra height splits evenly above and below the text, as CSS half-leading does.
- `width` and `flex` on the direct children of a `flex-direction: row` box, which is where columns are measured. Both reject anywhere else rather than being dropped. The `flex` shorthand keeps only the grow factor; grow 0 needs a `width`. A column cannot keep a basis, so a grow above 0 with a length basis (`flex: 1 1 100px`), or with `auto` beside a `width`, rejects. `flex: 1` has a zero basis, so a `width` beside it is ignored, as in CSS.
- `border-style` and `border-top-style` to `border-left-style`, or the style word in `border` and the side shorthands: `none`, `hidden`, `solid`, `dashed`, `dotted`, set per side. `double`, `groove`, `ridge`, `inset`, and `outset` reject. A border with no style is solid, where browsers draw none, so `border-width` and `border-color` alone still draw a rule. Dashes follow Chrome's proportions: from a 3pt border up they are two widths long with one width between, below it three and two. Dots are round caps one width across with one width between. Each dashed or dotted side stretches its gaps so whole dashes fill it and the side starts and ends on a dash. Top and bottom borders run the full width and left and right borders run between them, so the corners belong to the top and bottom.
- `border-radius` and `border-top-left-radius`, `border-top-right-radius`, `border-bottom-right-radius`, `border-bottom-left-radius`. A corner takes one or two lengths or percents, horizontal then vertical, and the shorthand takes one to four corners with an optional `/` and one to four vertical radii, as in CSS (`4pt 8pt / 50% 2pt`). A percent is of the box's width for the horizontal radius and its height for the vertical one. Radii that overrun a side shrink together by one factor, as CSS Backgrounds 3 says, so `9999px` and Tailwind's `rounded-full` make a pill. The background fills the rounded shape and the border follows it as one stroke along its centre line. That needs the same width, colour, and style on all four borders, or no border; anything else rejects, because a rounded corner has no mitre between two different sides. A radius on `img` or `svg` rejects: the picture would stay rectangular. Children are not clipped to the corners, as with `overflow: visible`.
- `opacity` on any element: a number or percent, clamped to 0 through 1 as CSS clamps it. It multiplies down the tree, and each box, line of text, underline, and image draws with its own `/ExtGState` alpha. That is a per-element approximation, not a transparency group, so the parts of one translucent box do not composite as a unit. A card's background shows through its text, and a dark card with light text reads flatter than in a browser. A drawing (`svg`, or `img` with SVG) folds the opacity into each shape. `opacity` on a `header` or `footer` rejects. PDF/A-3b accepts the output; veraPDF 1.30 validates it with tagging on.
- Vertical margins collapse as in CSS 2.1 section 8.3.1. Adjoining margins become one, the largest of them: those of adjacent siblings, of a box and its first or last child when no top or bottom border or padding separates them, and the top and bottom margins of an empty block. Negative margins reject, so there is nothing to subtract. At an unforced page break the margin is truncated, so the block after the break starts at the top of the page; a forced break drops the margin before it and keeps the one after it. Flex items and table cells keep their own margins, also at a page break, and never collapse them with their siblings', their container's, or their children's.
- Margin, padding, borders, solid backgrounds, text color, `font-family`, and size. `font-weight` takes `normal`, `bold`, or a number from 1 to 1000, where 600 and up is bold; anything else, `bolder` and `lighter` included, rejects.
- `font-style: normal | italic | oblique`. `oblique` picks the italic face too, and an angle rejects. The built-in fonts have their own italic faces: Helvetica-Oblique, Times-Italic, and Courier-Oblique, each with a bold version. An embedded family uses its `italic` or `boldItalic` file; without one, FlashPDF slants the upright face by a quarter (about 14 degrees), as browsers do under `font-synthesis`. A bold italic without a `boldItalic` file slants the bold face.
- `font-kerning: auto | normal | none`, kerning by default. Adjacent letters of a word kern when they share a font and size, also across inline elements; pairs next to a space or a CJK character do not. Helvetica and Times use the KPX pairs of Adobe's AFM files for every WinAnsi pair; Courier is fixed-width and has none. An embedded font uses GPOS pair adjustment (lookup type 2, formats 1 and 2, also inside extension lookups) under the `kern` feature of the `DFLT` script, else `dflt` or `latn`, taking the first glyph's x-advance adjustment; without that, its `kern` table's format 0 subtables. FlashPDF reads at most 128 pair subtables per font. Kerning counts when lines break.
- `text-align: left | center | right | justify`. `justify` stretches the spaces and no-break spaces of every wrapped line to the full width; the last line and a line ending in `<br>` stay left-aligned, and a line without a space, such as unspaced CJK, stays left-aligned too.
- `letter-spacing` and `word-spacing` as `normal` or a length, negative allowed, up to 14,400pt either way; percentages reject. Letter spacing follows every character, including the last on a line, as in browsers. Word spacing follows every space and no-break space. Both count when lines break, and both inherit as points, so `0.1em` resolves against the element that declares it. Embedded fonts ignore the PDF word-spacing operator, so FlashPDF positions each space itself.
- `text-transform: none | uppercase | lowercase | capitalize`, applied before layout with locale-independent case mapping, so Turkish dotted i follows the default Unicode rules. `capitalize` uppercases the first letter of each word, where a word starts after anything but a letter, digit, or mark, except an apostrophe, period, colon, or middle dot inside a word: `don't` stays one word and `e-mail` becomes `E-Mail`.
- `text-decoration: none | underline | line-through`, alone or combined, optionally with `solid` and a colour, on any text element, and `text-decoration-color`. Descendants inherit both, and a shorthand without a colour resets it to the text colour. An underline is centred on the font's underline position at its thickness: the built-in fonts' AFM values (-100 and 50 per 1000), or an embedded font's `post` table. A line-through uses the `OS/2` strikeout, or half the AFM x-height. `overline` and the `dotted`, `dashed`, `double`, and `wavy` styles reject.
- `img` and `svg`: `width` anywhere (`pt`, `px`, `rem`, `%`) and `height` (`pt`, `px`, `rem`), which only they accept. PNG at 8 bits per channel: greyscale, RGB, and palette pass through undecoded with `/Predictor 15`; greyscale+alpha, RGBA, and a palette with `tRNS` split the alpha into an `/SMask`; a greyscale or RGB `tRNS` colour key becomes `/Mask`. JPEG baseline and progressive pass through as `/DCTDecode` in greyscale, RGB, or CMYK, with Adobe APP14 CMYK inverted through `/Decode`.
- `white-space: normal | nowrap | pre | pre-wrap | pre-line`, inherited; `vertical-align: baseline | sub | super` on inline elements; and `list-style-type`, `list-style-position`, and the `list-style` shorthand, inherited.
- `a`: `href` as an absolute `http:`, `https:`, or `mailto:` URL, or `#id`, emitted as one `/Link` annotation per line segment or image. An `http:` or `https:` URL must spell out its host, so `http:foo` rejects. `font-family` lists are read in order and the first entry that is a registered `RenderOptions.fonts` family or a built-in name wins; a registered family beats a built-in of the same name. The built-in names are `Helvetica`, `Arial`, `sans-serif`, `system-ui`, and `ui-sans-serif` for Helvetica, `Times`, `Times New Roman`, `serif`, and `ui-serif` for Times, and `Courier`, `Courier New`, `monospace`, and `ui-monospace` for Courier. Names are case-insensitive, and a quoted `'serif'` is a family name, not the generic keyword. Any other entry, such as `Georgia` or `Inter`, is skipped for the next one, as in a browser. A list where no entry matches rejects with `unregistered font family`, naming the list, so a typo or a forgotten `fonts` entry never silently changes the typeface; end a list with `serif`, `sans-serif`, or `monospace` to always match. Colours accept hex, `rgb()`, `hsl()`, `oklch()`, `oklab()`, `currentcolor`, and CSS named colours; OKLab colours outside sRGB clip per channel, and a colour with alpha rejects; use `opacity` on the element.

- Browser defaults give `p` vertical margins of `1em`, and `h1`-`h6` vertical margins of `0.67em`/`0.83em`/`1em`/`1.33em`/`1.67em`/`2.33em`, measured against each element's font size. `hr` has `0.5em` vertical margins and `th` centres its text. Matching rules and inline styles override these defaults; adjoining vertical margins collapse.
- `h1`-`h6` defaults of `2em`/`1.5em`/`1.17em`/`1em`/`0.83em`/`0.67em` and bold, which a matching rule or inline style overrides but an inherited `font-size` does not.
- `break-before`, `break-after`, and `break-inside: avoid`.
- Tables: `width` and `flex` on the first row's `th`/`td` size the columns; `padding`, `border`, `background`, and text properties on cells; `background` on `tr`; `width`, `margin-top`, `margin-bottom`, and `break-inside: avoid` on `table`; `break-inside: avoid` on `tbody` keeps its rows together.

Normal block children stream independently, so long `<main>` documents paginate without retaining the whole layout. Flex rows and `break-inside: avoid` groups remain atomic. A single open container may buffer at most 16 MiB of layout commands, and a document may paint at most 16 MiB of content on at most 10,000 pages.

## Intentional v1 limits

- Helvetica, Times, and Courier, each in regular, bold, italic or oblique, and bold italic, are the built-in fonts, and Helvetica is the default. Registered host-provided TTF families support regular and optional bold, italic, and bold italic files. Variable-face selection, OpenType features other than kerning, shaping, and `text-decoration: overline` reject.
- Text in an embedded TTF renders any character the font has a glyph for, including CJK and supplementary-plane characters; a character without a glyph rejects, naming it and its code point. There is no shaping, ligature substitution, or bidi: each character maps to one glyph, combining marks render as spacing glyphs, and right-to-left text comes out in logical order. The built-in faces are WinAnsi standard fonts, so non-Latin text needs an embedded font. They have no embedded program, so PDF/A rejects all twelve. Lines break at ASCII whitespace and between CJK characters; U+00A0 does not break.
- Images: 16-bit, 1/2/4-bit, and interlaced PNGs, 12-bit, lossless, hierarchical, and arithmetic-coded JPEGs, and every other format (GIF, WebP) reject naming the feature. A PNG with transparency must decode to at most 64 MiB, and all images in one render are capped at 64 MiB of input. `object-fit` rejects: an image always scales to its box. An image wider than its container or taller than an empty page rejects with the element path. Image bytes are not CRC-checked, and a PNG without transparency passes through without being inflated, so render time stays proportional to the file size; a corrupt file produces a corrupt picture, not an error.
- SVG: text, gradients, patterns, filters, masks, `clipPath`, `use`, `symbol`, `defs`, `image`, `foreignObject`, `<style>` and classes inside a drawing, and any other element, attribute, or style property reject with a reason. `opacity` multiplies into each shape's fill and stroke opacity instead of compositing the group, so overlapping shapes in a translucent `g` show through each other. A drawing holds at most 10,000 elements and 100,000 path segments. A percent `width` together with a `height` rejects unless `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, because the box's aspect ratio is unknown before layout. SVG files may hold XML's five named entities and numeric ones; comments and the XML declaration are skipped, and a DOCTYPE with an internal subset, CDATA, and `class` or `style` on the root reject. Design tools often add editor metadata or a `<style>` block, so run their files through SVGO first.
- Links: internal `#anchor` links are not supported yet, and `javascript:`, relative, and non-ASCII-after-encoding URLs reject. Link rectangles have no border. Footer text takes no links or decoration.
- No gradients or background images (`background-image`, or `linear-gradient()` in `background`), `box-shadow`, `outline`, `border-image`, `height` outside `img`, `min-width`, or `max-width`. `border-top`, `border-right`, `border-bottom`, and `border-left` accept the same syntax as `border`; `<hr>` renders a one-point black bottom rule, which its own border declarations override; it takes `break-before` and `break-after`, and `width` only as a flex row child.
- Images: 16-bit, 1/2/4-bit, and interlaced PNGs, 12-bit, lossless, hierarchical, and arithmetic-coded JPEGs, and every other format (GIF, WebP, SVG) reject naming the feature. A PNG with transparency must decode to at most 64 MiB, and all images in one render are capped at 64 MiB of input. `object-fit` rejects: an image always scales to its box. An image wider than its container or taller than an empty page rejects with the element path. Image bytes are not CRC-checked; a corrupt file produces a corrupt picture, not an error.
- Links: `javascript:`, relative, and non-ASCII-after-encoding URLs reject, and so does a `#id` that is empty or not printable ASCII. Link rectangles have no border. Footer text takes no links or decoration. A destination always sits at the top of a line, so an empty element's id points at the line after it. Only ids that links name become named destinations, so `#nameddest=` cannot reach an id no link uses.
- No border radius, gradients, `height` outside `img`, `min-width`, or `max-width`. `border-top`, `border-right`, `border-bottom`, and `border-left` accept the same solid syntax as `border`; `<hr>` renders a one-point black bottom rule, which its own border declarations override; it takes `break-before` and `break-after`, and `width` only as a flex row child.
- Tables have no `colSpan`/`rowSpan`, `colgroup`/`col`, or `caption`. `vertical-align` rejects: cells align to the top. `border-collapse` and `border-spacing` reject: cells tile the row with separate borders, so adjacent full borders double up; draw one edge per cell (`border-bottom`, and `border-left` on all but the first column) for single rules. `table` takes no padding, border, background, or horizontal margins; style its cells or a wrapping `div`. A table inside a decorated box, a flex row, or a `break-inside: avoid` group is atomic like any other content there.
- A decorated box is atomic. Use document margins for page-wide padding and decorate individual cards/sections, not a multi-page root wrapper. Unmatched rules and at-rules other than `@media` and `@property` are skipped silently; `@layer` and `@supports` block contents are skipped too, so import `tailwindcss/utilities` rather than the full preflight. A rule whose selector can match nothing in a PDF is skipped silently as well: a pseudo-element (`::before`, `::after`, `::placeholder`, `::selection`, `::backdrop`), a pseudo-class of user or form state (`:hover`, `:focus`, `:active`, `:visited`, `:focus-visible`, `:disabled`, `:checked`, and the like), `:host`, and a type FlashPDF never renders. Inside `:not()` such a part is simply false, so `:not(:hover)` matches every element, and Tailwind's `hover:` and `focus:` do nothing. Every other selector outside the subset rejects with its reason: attribute selectors (Tailwind's `data-*:` and `aria-*:`), `:has()`, `:lang()`, `:dir()`, `:scope`, `:link`, `:any-link`, `:defined`, `:nth-*(... of <selector>)`, namespaces (`*|a`), unknown pseudo-classes, and malformed selectors. When a selector holds both kinds, such as `a[href]::after`, it can never match and is skipped.
