# FlashPDF CSS compatibility

FlashPDF renders a static CSS subset. It does not embed a browser engine, fetch assets, execute JavaScript in CSS, or support container queries, Grid, sticky positioning, transforms, filters, animations, or SVG layout beyond the supported inline drawing subset. `@media` supports `print`, `screen`, and width conditions only.

The public authoring API is native JSX plus `render`. `PageNumber` and `TotalPages` work anywhere inside the `header`, `footer`, `firstPageHeader`, and `firstPageFooter` options, and the characters they stand for, U+001E and U+001F, reject in your own text. There are no `Document`, `Text`, `Stack`, `Row`, preview, or download helpers; tables use the HTML `table` tags.

## Authoring

FlashPDF supports a small static JSX vocabulary, not browser HTML compatibility. Use `main`, `div`, `section`, `article`, `header`, `footer`, `nav`, `aside`, `p`, headings, `pre`, `blockquote`, `address`, `figure`, `figcaption`, `ul`, `ol`, `li`, `dl`, `dt`, `dd`, `span`, `b`, `strong`, `em`, `i`, `cite`, `small`, `sub`, `sup`, `u`, `ins`, `s`, `del`, `mark`, `code`, `kbd`, `samp`, `a`, `br`, `hr`, `img`, and `svg`. A paragraph is a sequence of inline runs: adjacent text and the inline elements (`span` through `samp`, and `a`) flow together and wrap as one, each run keeping its own font, weight, style, size, and colour, so `<p>Hello <b>World</b></p>` is one line. `em`, `i`, and `cite` are italic by default, as in browsers. A mixed line takes the tallest run's height. `br` forces a line break; `br` and `hr` take no children, and `br` takes no box or layout styles. Whitespace collapses to one space across runs and is trimmed at line ends. Whitespace-only text between block-level children or flex items, such as Prettier's `{" "}`, renders nothing, as in CSS. An inline element with margin, padding, border, background, `width`, `flex`, or its own `text-align` remains a standalone layout node; `mark` is the exception for its background, below. A word wider than its column breaks at the last glyph that fits. [Page breaks](#page-breaks) describes where content splits across pages.

Tables use `table`, `thead`, `tbody`, `tfoot`, `tr`, `th`, and `td`. Each column takes its width from its first cell without `colSpan`, looking through `thead`, then `tbody`, then `tfoot` rows: `width` as an absolute length or `%`, or `flex`, exactly as flex-row children; columns without either share the remainder equally. So a header row whose first cell spans two columns leaves those two columns to the first body row. A cell with `colSpan` takes the summed widths of its columns and rejects `width` and `flex`. `rowSpan` joins rows inside one `thead`, `tbody`, or `tfoot`, and adjacent bare `tr` rows form one group, as HTML's implied `tbody` does; a span past the group's last row rejects. Every row must fill the table's columns, counting the ones spans cover, and a `td` or `th` outside a table row rejects. A cell holds text, inline runs, or nested blocks; a row is as tall as its tallest cell and every cell stretches to it, so cell backgrounds and borders tile the row. When a `rowSpan` cell needs more height than its rows, its last row grows. `vertical-align: top | middle | bottom` on a cell, or on its `tr` or row group, places the cell's content in that height; cells default to `top`, where browsers default to `middle`. A table splits between rows: `thead` rows repaint at the top of every page it continues on, `tfoot` rows repaint after the last body row of every page, and a row that does not fit moves whole to the next page under the repeated header. Rows that a `rowSpan` joins move together, like a `break-inside: avoid` group, and reject naming their first row when they are taller than the page beside the repeated header and footer. `th` is bold and centred by default. A `td`/`th` accepts padding, border, background, `vertical-align`, and text properties; a `tr` accepts a background that paints under its cells; a `table` accepts `width`, vertical margins, `break-inside: avoid`, and `border-collapse`. In rows that a `rowSpan` joins, a `tr` background paints in each cell that starts in that row, and other `tr` box styles reject. Text wraps inside a narrow cell rather than clipping.

`border-collapse: collapse` draws each line between two cells once. Per edge, the widest of the two cells' borders and their rows' borders wins; on a tie a cell's border beats its row's, and the upper or left one beats the other, as CSS 2.1 resolves conflicts. Each cell draws half of a shared edge, so the line centres on the cell boundary, and all of an outer edge, inside the table. A repeated `thead` or `tfoot` keeps its half of the edge it shares with the first or last body row. `border-collapse` and `border-spacing` inherit, as in CSS.

```tsx
<table style={{ width: "100%" }}>
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Item</th>
      <th style={{ width: "80pt", textAlign: "right" }}>Amount</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    {items.map((item) => (
      <tr key={item.id}>
        <td>{item.name}</td>
        <td style={{ textAlign: "right" }}>{item.total}</td>
      </tr>
    ))}
  </tbody>
</table>
```

Lists use `ul`, `ol`, and `li`. A `ul` or `ol` takes only `li` children, and an `li` belongs in one. Browser defaults apply: 40px of left padding, `1em` vertical margins that drop to zero in a nested list, and `list-style-type` `disc` for a `ul`, then `circle` and `square` one and two lists deeper, and `decimal` for an `ol`. `list-style-type` takes `disc`, `circle`, `square`, `decimal`, `decimal-leading-zero`, `lower-alpha`, `lower-latin`, `upper-alpha`, `upper-latin`, `lower-roman`, `upper-roman`, and `none`. `list-style-position` takes `outside`, the default, or `inside`. The `list-style` shorthand sets both and, as in CSS, resets a part it omits, so `list-style: inside` on an `ol` brings back `disc`. `ol` takes `start` and `reversed`, and `li` takes `value` in an `ol` only. A number outside its counter's range, such as zero in `lower-alpha` or 4000 in `lower-roman`, falls back to decimal. The marker belongs to the first line of the item's first paragraph and moves with it across pages. An outside marker hangs left of the item's text, right-aligned against it, in the item's font, size, and colour; an inside marker starts the first line. Bullets are shapes the engine draws, not glyphs, so they need no glyph in the font and work under PDF/A; counters are text, so their digits and letters must exist in the font. An item that begins with a nested list, table, or image puts its marker on a line of its own. `list-style-image`, string markers, and other counter styles reject. In tagged mode a list item is a paragraph and its counter is a second one: there is no `L`, `LI`, `Lbl`, or `LBody` structure yet.

Images use `img` with `src` as the PNG, JPEG, or SVG file bytes in a `Uint8Array`, passed by the host exactly like fonts: FlashPDF fetches nothing, and a string path or `data:` URL rejects with a hint. In tagged mode, nonempty `alt` becomes the figure description and empty `alt` marks a decorative image. An image is an atomic block box, left-aligned in its container. `width` and `height` take an absolute length, or (width only) `%` of the container width; given one, the other follows the image's aspect ratio; given neither, the pixel size at 96 px per inch applies, capped to the container width. `max-width` (an absolute length or `%` of the container width) and `max-height` (an absolute length) cap each axis; with `width` and `height` both auto the image scales into both caps and keeps its aspect ratio, as CSS 2.1 §10.4 says, so `maxWidth: '100%', maxHeight: '277mm'` fits a scan on an A4 page with 10mm margins. A cap never enlarges an image. Images work as block children, in table cells, in flex rows, and in decorated boxes, and a paragraph splits around them at page ends, but an image cannot sit inside a paragraph's text. The same `Uint8Array` used on every row embeds once.

`dl`, `dt`, `dd`, `blockquote`, `address`, `figure`, `figcaption`, `nav`, and `aside` are blocks with the browser defaults: `blockquote` and `figure` have `1em 40px` margins, `dl` has `1em` vertical margins, `dd` indents 40px, and `address` is italic. Vertical margins collapse, so two `blockquote`s in a row are `1em` apart, as in a browser.

`pre` is a monospace block with `1em` vertical margins and `white-space: pre`. `white-space` takes `normal`, `nowrap`, `pre`, `pre-wrap`, and `pre-line`, and anything else rejects. `pre` and `pre-wrap` keep spaces and newlines, `pre-line` keeps newlines and collapses spaces, and `nowrap` collapses whitespace and never wraps. A tab moves to the next multiple of 8 characters from the line start; the count is in characters, so a proportional font only approximates it. A `pre` or `nowrap` line wider than its container rejects with `LineTooWide` and the element path, where `pre-wrap` wraps it. One newline right after `<pre>` is dropped, as in HTML, so a template literal can start on its own line. The engine collapses ASCII spaces, so `pre` and `pre-wrap` keep them as U+00A0: a font without that glyph shows its space glyph, and copied text has no-break spaces. A paragraph record holds 64 KiB, which a `pre` of about 800 average lines fills; split a longer listing into several `pre` blocks with `margin: 0`.

`small`, `sub`, and `sup` are `0.83em`. `sub` and `sup` shift the baseline down by a fifth or up by a third of the parent's font size, and nested ones add up. `vertical-align` takes `baseline`, `sub`, and `super` on inline elements; It rejects on blocks and on inline elements with box styles; table cells instead accept `top`, `middle`, and `bottom`. `u` and `ins` underline, `s` and `del` strike through, `code`, `kbd`, and `samp` use the `monospace` family, and `mark` is black on yellow. A `mark` background paints behind its text, one rectangle per line segment covering the font's ascent and descent; it is the only inline element whose background does not make a layout node.

Vector drawings use `svg` and lay out exactly like `img`: the `width` and `height` attributes are the intrinsic size in CSS pixels, style `width` and `height` resize the box, and the drawing is clipped to the box. Without `width` and `height`, the `viewBox` size in pixels applies; without a `viewBox`, the drawing scales as if it were `0 0 width height`. `viewBox` and `preserveAspectRatio` (`none`, or `xMinYMin` to `xMaxYMax` with `meet` or `slice`) map the drawing into the box. Inside go `g`, `rect` with `rx`/`ry`, `circle`, `ellipse`, `line`, `polyline`, `polygon`, and `path` with the full `d` grammar (relative commands, `H`/`V`, `S`/`T`, `Q`, and arcs, which become cubic curves), plus `title` and `desc`. Every element takes `transform` (`matrix`, `translate`, `scale`, `rotate`, `skewX`, `skewY`) and the presentation attributes `fill`, `fill-rule`, `fill-opacity`, `stroke`, `stroke-width`, `stroke-linecap`, `stroke-linejoin`, `stroke-miterlimit`, `stroke-dasharray`, `stroke-dashoffset`, `stroke-opacity`, and `opacity`, under their React names (`strokeWidth`) or SVG names. A `style` object or declaration string on a shape or `g` takes the same properties and wins over the attributes. Colours take the CSS colour syntax plus `none` and `currentColor`, the element's CSS `color`. In tagged mode, `aria-label` or the first `<title>` becomes the figure description, and a drawing with neither is an artifact. The same drawing on every row embeds once. `QRCode` and `Barcode` return an `svg`, so they size, clip, and tag the same way. `<img src={svgBytes}>` takes an SVG file with the same subset: FlashPDF parses the markup itself and needs no DOM.

```tsx
<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" width={32} aria-label="Paid" style={{ color: "#0a7" }}>
  <circle cx={12} cy={12} r={10} fill="none" stroke="currentColor" strokeWidth={2} />
  <path d="M7 12l3 3 7-7" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" strokeLinecap="round" />
</svg>
```

Links use `a` with an absolute `http:`, `https:`, or `mailto:` `href`, or `#id` to jump to the element with that `id`. Inside a paragraph an `a` is an inline run like `b`; around an `img` or a block it makes that content clickable. Every wrapped line of a link gets its own rectangle. `a` defaults to `color: #0000EE` and `text-decoration: underline`, and a rule or inline style overrides both.

```tsx
<a href="https://example.com/invoices/42">
  <img src={logoBytes} alt="Acme Supply Co." style={{ width: 120 }} />
</a>
<p>
  Questions? <a href="mailto:billing@example.com">Email billing</a>
</p>
```

A link to `#id` is a `GoTo` action to a destination at the top of the target's first line, at the left edge of its content box, with the viewer's zoom unchanged. The destination sits on the page where that line lands, so a target that moves to the next page takes its destination with it. Every target is also a named destination, so `invoice.pdf#nameddest=terms` opens at `<section id="terms">` in a viewer. The id is matched exactly: printable ASCII, 1 to 255 characters, no percent-decoding. An element can be a target when it is a block (`div`, `main`, `section`, `article`, `header`, `footer`), `p`, a heading, `img`, `hr`, `table`, `td`, `th`, or a block-level `span` or `a`. An id that a link names must be on exactly one element. Ids that no link names stay free for CSS selectors and may repeat. A link to a missing or repeated id, or to an id on an inline `span`, `b`, `strong`, or `a` inside a paragraph, a `br`, a `tr`, or a table row group, rejects naming the id.

`RenderOptions.outline` bookmarks every `h1` to `h6` in the body, in document order. A heading nests under the nearest earlier heading with a lower level, so an `h3` after an `h1` sits directly under it. The title is the heading's text with whitespace collapsed, cut to 255 characters; a heading with no text gets no bookmark. `data-bookmark="Title"` on a heading replaces the title, and `data-bookmark={false}` leaves the heading out. Headings in a header or footer never get one. A PDF with bookmarks opens with the outline panel showing. Link targets and bookmarks share a limit of 10,000 per render.

```tsx
<h2 id="terms" data-bookmark="Terms of payment">Terms</h2>
<p>See the <a href="#terms">terms</a>.</p>
```

Compile CSS in the consuming app's build step, then pass the resulting string to `render`. CSS Modules and minification belong to that build step. `stylesheet` validates the final CSS against FlashPDF's supported subset.

```ts
import { render } from '@pettersen3008/flashpdf';

const css = '.invoice { padding: 18pt; background: #fff }';
const pdf = await render(<main className="invoice">Invoice</main>, {
  stylesheets: [css],
});
```

The CSS may come from CSS Modules or any tool that emits static CSS. Runnable build-step examples cover [Tailwind](./packages/flashpdf/examples/css-integrations/tailwind.mjs), [StyleX](./packages/flashpdf/examples/css-integrations/stylex.mjs), and [styled-components](./packages/flashpdf/examples/css-integrations/styled-components.mjs). Run all three with `pnpm --filter @pettersen3008/flashpdf examples:css`.

Tailwind's example imports `tailwindcss/theme.css` with `theme(inline)`, so colour and spacing utilities carry their values instead of `var()` references to a `:root` rule in `@layer theme`, which FlashPDF skips, and `tailwindcss/utilities.css` with `source(none)`, so it builds only the listed utilities, unlayered. It renders an invoice from that build; the Tailwind v4 section of the [guide](./docs/guide.md#tailwind-v4) lists the utilities that render and the ones that reject. StyleX passes Babel metadata through `processStylexRules`. styled-components' server output also contains hydration metadata with unsupported attribute selectors and `content`; its example selects the generated component rule before calling `render`. FlashPDF adds no runtime adapter for any of them.

Unsupported declarations fail at `render` only for rules that match an element, naming the property, selector, and source position. A sheet passed through `stylesheet(source)` gets a full check: the first `render` that uses it validates the properties and values of every rule with a supported selector, matched or not, and a later render of the same text skips the check once it passed. Custom property definitions and values that use `var()`, `calc()`, `min()`, `max()`, `clamp()`, or `color-mix()` are checked by `render` where a rule applies, since only the cascade resolves them and Tailwind puts them in most utilities. Both also validate `@page` rules. Property names are case-insensitive, and a camelCase name such as `fontSize` rejects with its kebab-case spelling:

```
unsupported CSS property: box-shadow in selector ".total" at 4:3
```

At-rules other than `@media`, `@property`, and `@page` are skipped. So is a rule whose selector can match nothing in a PDF, such as `:hover`, `::before`, or `button`. A selector that could match an element but is outside the subset, such as `[href]` or `:has()`, rejects at `render`, naming the selector and position, whether or not any element matches:

```
unsupported CSS selector: :has() (not implemented) in selector ".card:has(.badge)" at 2:1
```

Every runtime error names the element and its ancestors, for example `on <span.total> in <div.row> > <main.invoice>`. FlashPDF never drops a declaration that applies to an element it renders.

## Page breaks

FlashPDF follows [CSS Fragmentation Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/) for the properties below. A page can break between sibling blocks, between table rows, and between the lines of a paragraph. It never breaks between a box's top border and padding and its first child, or between its last child and its bottom padding and border, so no page ends with only a box's top edge or starts with only its bottom edge.

- A box that does not fit the space left breaks at the page end, like CSS `box-decoration-break: slice`. The first page draws its top border and padding, the last page its bottom ones, and every page its side borders, side padding, and background, with the content continuing below the page header. Nested boxes break the same way.
- `orphans` and `widows`, 2 by default and inherited, are the fewest lines of a paragraph a break leaves at the bottom of a page and carries to the top of the next. A paragraph that cannot keep both moves whole. `orphans: 1; widows: 1` breaks wherever the page ends.
- `break-inside: avoid` keeps a box, paragraph, table, or `tbody` on one page: it moves to the next page, and rejects when taller than an empty page. A flex row that does not wrap and a table row never break inside; a wrapping flex row breaks between its rows.
- `break-before` and `break-after` take `auto`, `page`, `avoid`, and `avoid-page`. `page` forces a break, also inside a box, which continues on the next page; a forced break with nothing above it on its page does nothing, so two in a row make one. `avoid` keeps an element with the content on the other side of that break: a heading with the first lines of the next paragraph, honouring `orphans`, a table row with the next row, or a paragraph's last `widows` lines with the block after it. A first child's `break-before` and a last child's `break-after` apply to their parent's edge too, as CSS propagates them.
- `h1` to `h6` default to `break-after: avoid`, as print style sheets do: the CSS 2.1 sample style sheet, Prince, and WeasyPrint. The HTML Standard's rendering rules and browsers' style sheets set no break properties, so a browser prints a heading alone at the bottom of a page; `break-after: auto` restores that.
- Margins next to an unforced break truncate to zero. After a forced break they remain.
- `avoid-page` means `avoid`, because a page is the only fragment. `left`, `right`, `recto`, and `verso` reject: pages have no sides.

When the rules leave no break point on a page, FlashPDF drops `orphans` and `widows` first and then the avoid rules, as CSS allows, instead of losing content. It decides with at most one page of content held back: content after the page's last allowed break point waits, unpainted, until the next allowed break point arrives. A run of avoided breaks taller than a page, or holding more than 16 MiB of layout, therefore breaks anyway.

## Supported subset

- Selectors: tags (case-insensitive, as in HTML), `*`, `.class`, `#id`, and compounds of them, joined by the descendant, child `>`, next-sibling `+`, and subsequent-sibling `~` combinators. Class and id names may use CSS escapes, so `.w-1\/2`, `.w-\[120px\]`, and `.\32xl\:p-4` match the classes `w-1/2`, `w-[120px]`, and `2xl:p-4`.
- Pseudo-classes: `:root`, `:empty`, `:first-child`, `:last-child`, `:only-child`, `:nth-child()`, `:nth-last-child()`, the same five with `-of-type`, and `:is()`, `:where()`, and `:not()` with a selector list, complex selectors included, nested up to 16 levels. `:nth-*()` takes `An+B`, `odd`, or `even`; `of <selector>` rejects. These are what Tailwind's `first:`, `last:`, `only:`, `odd:`, `even:`, `empty:`, `space-y-*`, and `divide-y` compile to.
- Element siblings are counted on the resolved tree: components, fragments, arrays, `null`, and `false` have flattened away, and text does not count. The siblings of an element are the other element children of its parent, so in `<div>Total <p /> <p /></div>` the first `<p>` is `:first-child` and the second is `:nth-child(2)`. `:empty` matches an element whose children are all zero-length text, so a single space makes it non-empty. The top-level elements of the render input are siblings of each other, each one is `:root`, and the `header` and `footer` options are each a root of their own.
- Specificity as an (ids, classes, tags) tuple, then source order across the whole `stylesheets` array, so a later sheet wins a tie. `:is()` and `:not()` count their most specific argument, `:where()` counts nothing, and every other pseudo-class counts as a class. Inline styles beat both, and `!important` declarations beat all non-important ones. A shorthand and its longhands cascade in that order too, so a later `border: none` clears an earlier `border-bottom`, and a later `background` replaces `background-color`. An inline value of `undefined` is skipped, as React does.
- Matching cost is polynomial in the selector and the tree. Each descendant, subsequent-sibling, and nested-selector step caches one verdict per element, so a long selector on a deep or wide tree does not backtrack.
- `calc()`, `min()`, `max()`, and `clamp()` over lengths and plain numbers, evaluated after `var()` substitution with `+ - * /`, CSS precedence, and parentheses. `+`, `-`, `min()`, `max()`, and `clamp()` take operands of one kind (number, length, or percentage), `*` needs a number on one side, and `/` a nonzero number on the right. `em` resolves against the element's font size, and against the parent's inside `font-size`. A percentage result works for `width`, `height`, and `line-height`, and only when the whole expression is a percentage: `calc(1 / 2 * 100%)` is `50%`, but `calc(100% - 4pt)` rejects because the engine resolves widths later. An expression holds at most 1024 characters and 16 nested levels, and only a call at the top of a value is evaluated, so `rgb(calc(...))` rejects.
- Logical properties for horizontal left-to-right text: `margin-inline`, `margin-block`, `padding-inline`, and `padding-block` take one value, or two as start then end, and each has `-start` and `-end` longhands. Inline start is left, inline end is right, block start is top, and block end is bottom, and a later physical longhand overrides them in cascade order. They do not follow `direction`, vertical writing modes do not exist, and `border-inline*` and `border-block*` (Tailwind's `border-x` and `border-y`) reject.
- `border-style` and the per-side `border-top-style`, `border-right-style`, `border-bottom-style`, and `border-left-style` take `solid`, `none`, or `hidden`. `none` and `hidden` clear the width whatever the declaration order, and `dotted`, `dashed`, `double`, `groove`, `ridge`, `inset`, and `outset` reject. The per-side `border-*-width` and `border-*-color` work too. A width with no style draws solid, unlike a browser, where the style defaults to `none`, and a style with no width draws nothing.
- `@media` with `print` or `all` applies and `screen` is skipped. `(width >= 48rem)`, `(width < 48rem)`, `(min-width: 30rem)`, and `(max-width: 30rem)` compare the page content width, which is the page width minus both margins, in points; `rem` is 12 pt. They combine with `and`, `,`, `not`, and `only`, and `@media` blocks nest 16 levels deep. With A4 and the default 36 pt margin the content is 523 pt, so Tailwind's `sm:` (480 pt) applies and `md:` (576 pt) does not until the margin is 9.5 pt or less. Any other query, such as `(prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, rejects with its reason where a rule inside it matches an element, and `stylesheet()` accepts it.
- `@property` registers a custom property: `initial-value` is the value of an element that sets none, ahead of a `var()` fallback, and `inherits: false` stops inheritance. `syntax` is required but not checked. Tailwind's `--tw-border-style: solid` default works this way, so its `border` utility draws. The `@layer properties { @supports ... }` fallback for browsers without `@property` is skipped with every other `@layer` and `@supports` block.
- Inherited text properties and `var(--name, fallback)`. The fallback may hold functions and a nested `var()`, such as `var(--c, rgb(0, 0, 0))` or `var(--a, var(--b))`.
- `inherit` and `initial` on every supported property. `inherit` takes the parent's value, and an `em` length the parent declared resolves again against this element's font size. `currentcolor` is the element's own `color`.
- Absolute lengths in `pt`, `px`, `pc`, `in`, `cm`, `mm`, and `Q` at CSS's fixed ratios (1in = 2.54cm = 25.4mm = 101.6Q = 96px = 72pt = 6pc), `em`, `rem`, unitless values where accepted, and percent widths in flex rows. `rem` is 12pt.
- Block flow, flex rows/columns, `gap`, `row-gap`, and `column-gap` between flex items, rows of a wrapping flex row, and column children, page size, orientation, and per-side document margins.
- `@page` with `size`, `margin`, and the four margin longhands. `size` takes `auto`, one or two lengths, or `A3`, `A4`, `A5`, `Letter`, or `Legal` with an optional `portrait` or `landscape`. Named sizes are whole points, as PostScript names them: A4 is 595 x 842. One size and one set of margins apply to every page, and an unset margin stays at 36pt.
- `@page` declarations cascade across the `stylesheets` array: a later one wins, and `!important` beats normal ones. `render` options beat both: `pageFormat` replaces the `@page` size, `orientation` turns whichever size applies, and `margin` replaces all four `@page` margins. Page selectors (`:first`, `:left`, `:right`, named pages), margin boxes such as `@top-center`, percent margins, other properties, and sides over 14,400pt reject with a reason. The `header` and `footer` options take the place of margin boxes, and `firstPageHeader` and `firstPageFooter` give the first page its own.
- `line-height` as `normal` (for the built-in fonts, the browser's: Helvetica and Times are 1.15em and Courier is 1.133em, the hhea ascent, descent, and line gap of Arial, Times New Roman, and Courier New, with the line gap split above and below the text; an embedded font uses its own metrics), a unitless factor, a percent, or a length. A factor inherits as a factor; a percent or `em` resolves against the element's own font size and inherits as a length. The extra height splits evenly above and below the text, as CSS half-leading does.
- `border-style` and `border-top-style` to `border-left-style`, or the style word in `border` and the side shorthands: `none`, `hidden`, `solid`, `dashed`, `dotted`, set per side. `double`, `groove`, `ridge`, `inset`, and `outset` reject. A border with no style is solid, where browsers draw none, so `border-width` and `border-color` alone still draw a rule. Dashes follow Chrome's proportions: from a 3pt border up they are two widths long with one width between, below it three and two. Dots are round caps one width across with one width between. Each dashed or dotted side stretches its gaps so whole dashes fill it and the side starts and ends on a dash. Top and bottom borders run the full width and left and right borders run between them, so the corners belong to the top and bottom.
- `border-radius` and `border-top-left-radius`, `border-top-right-radius`, `border-bottom-right-radius`, `border-bottom-left-radius`. A corner takes one or two lengths or percents, horizontal then vertical, and the shorthand takes one to four corners with an optional `/` and one to four vertical radii, as in CSS (`4pt 8pt / 50% 2pt`). A percent is of the box's width for the horizontal radius and its height for the vertical one. Radii that overrun a side shrink together by one factor, as CSS Backgrounds 3 says, so `9999px` and Tailwind's `rounded-full` make a pill. The background fills the rounded shape and the border follows it as one stroke along its centre line. That needs the same width, colour, and style on all four borders, or no border; anything else rejects, because a rounded corner has no mitre between two different sides. A radius on `img` or `svg` rejects: the picture would stay rectangular. Children are not clipped to the corners, as with `overflow: visible`.
- `opacity` on any element: a number or percent, clamped to 0 through 1 as CSS clamps it. It multiplies down the tree, and each box, line of text, underline, and image draws with its own `/ExtGState` alpha. That is a per-element approximation, not a transparency group, so the parts of one translucent box do not composite as a unit. A card's background shows through its text, and a dark card with light text reads flatter than in a browser. A drawing (`svg`, or `img` with SVG) folds the opacity into each shape. PDF/A-2b, PDF/A-2u, and PDF/A-3b accept the output; veraPDF 1.30 validates it with tagging on.
- Vertical margins collapse as in CSS 2.1 section 8.3.1. Adjoining margins become one, the largest of them: those of adjacent siblings, of a box and its first or last child when no top or bottom border or padding separates them, and the top and bottom margins of an empty block. Negative margins reject, so there is nothing to subtract. At an unforced page break the margin is truncated, so the block after the break starts at the top of the page; a forced break drops the margin before it and keeps the one after it. Flex items and table cells keep their own margins, also at a page break, and never collapse them with their siblings', their container's, or their children's.
- Margin, padding, borders, solid backgrounds, text color, `font-family`, and size. `font-weight` takes `normal`, `bold`, or a number from 1 to 1000; anything else, `bolder` and `lighter` included, rejects. A face with a `wght` axis takes the number exactly, and for the other faces 600 and up is bold.
- `font-style: normal | italic | oblique | oblique <angle>`, with the angle in `deg` from -90 to 90. `oblique` picks the italic face too. The built-in fonts have their own italic faces: Helvetica-Oblique, Times-Italic, and Courier-Oblique, each with a bold version. An embedded family uses its `italic` or `boldItalic` file; without one, a variable face takes `ital` 1 for `italic` or `slnt` at minus the angle for `oblique`, and a static face is slanted by a quarter (about 14 degrees), as browsers do under `font-synthesis`. A bold italic without a `boldItalic` file slants the bold face. An oblique angle other than 14 degrees, or a negative one, needs a `slnt` axis and otherwise rejects.
- <a id="variable-fonts"></a>Variable TrueType fonts (`fvar`, `gvar`, `avar`, `HVAR`, `MVAR`). `font-weight` sets `wght`, `font-stretch` (a percent, or `ultra-condensed` to `ultra-expanded`) sets `wdth`, and `font-style` sets `slnt` or `ital` as above. `font-variation-settings: "wght" 650, "wdth" 80` sets axes by their exact four-letter tag, overrides those properties, and takes the last value of a repeated tag; a tag the selected face has no axis for rejects, naming the face's axes, as does any setting on a static or built-in face. Values clamp to each axis's range, as CSS asks. A missing `bold` or `boldItalic` file gives way to a regular or italic file with a `wght` axis. Each set of axis values embeds as its own static TrueType subset: FlashPDF applies the `gvar` deltas in 16.16 fixed point, infers untouched points as the spec's IUP describes, moves composite components, takes advances from `HVAR` or the phantom points and line metrics from `MVAR`, drops the hinting, and names the `/BaseFont` after the values, such as `Inter-Regular_650wght`. HarfRust shapes at the same coordinates. `opsz` stays at its default because `font-optical-sizing` is not applied, and CFF2 outlines are not read.
- `font-kerning: auto | normal | none`, kerning by default. Helvetica and Times use the KPX pairs of Adobe's AFM files for every WinAnsi pair, also across inline elements that share the font and size; Courier is fixed-width and has none. An embedded font kerns through [shaping](#text-shaping): GPOS under the `kern` feature, else the `kern` table, and `none` turns the `kern` feature off. Pairs across a space or a line break opportunity do not kern. Kerning counts when lines break.
- `text-align: start | end | left | center | right | justify`, `start` by default, which is left in a left-to-right paragraph and right in a right-to-left one. `justify` stretches the spaces and no-break spaces of every wrapped line to the full width; the last line and a line ending in `<br>` align to the start, and a line without a space, such as unspaced CJK, does too.
- `direction: ltr | rtl`, inherited, `ltr` by default, and the `dir` attribute on any element, `ltr`, `rtl`, or `auto`. The paragraph takes its base direction from them, and `auto` takes it from the paragraph's first strong character. A CSS `direction` on the same element wins over `dir`, as over a browser's presentational hint. `direction` on an inline element without `unicode-bidi` changes nothing in CSS, and here too only the block's direction counts. List markers still hang on the left, and logical margins and paddings stay physical left and right.
- `letter-spacing` and `word-spacing` as `normal` or a length, negative allowed, up to 14,400pt either way; percentages reject. Letter spacing follows every character, including the last on a line, as in browsers. Word spacing follows every space and no-break space. Both count when lines break, and both inherit as points, so `0.1em` resolves against the element that declares it. Embedded fonts ignore the PDF word-spacing operator, so FlashPDF positions each space itself.
- `text-transform: none | uppercase | lowercase | capitalize`, applied before layout with locale-independent case mapping, so Turkish dotted i follows the default Unicode rules. `capitalize` uppercases the first letter of each word, where a word starts after anything but a letter, digit, or mark, except an apostrophe, period, colon, or middle dot inside a word: `don't` stays one word and `e-mail` becomes `E-Mail`.
- `text-decoration: none | underline | line-through`, alone or combined, optionally with `solid` and a colour, on any text element, and `text-decoration-color`. Descendants inherit both, and a shorthand without a colour resets it to the text colour. An underline is centred on the font's underline position at its thickness: the built-in fonts' AFM values (-100 and 50 per 1000), or an embedded font's `post` table. A line-through uses the `OS/2` strikeout, or half the AFM x-height. `overline` and the `dotted`, `dashed`, `double`, and `wavy` styles reject.
- `img` and `svg`: `width` and `max-width` as an absolute length or `%`, and `height` and `max-height` as an absolute length. PNG at 8 bits per channel: greyscale, RGB, and palette pass through undecoded with `/Predictor 15`; greyscale+alpha, RGBA, and a palette with `tRNS` split the alpha into an `/SMask`; a greyscale or RGB `tRNS` colour key becomes `/Mask`. JPEG baseline and progressive pass through as `/DCTDecode` in greyscale, RGB, or CMYK, with Adobe APP14 CMYK inverted through `/Decode`.
- `white-space: normal | nowrap | pre | pre-wrap | pre-line`, inherited; `vertical-align: baseline | sub | super` on inline elements; and `list-style-type`, `list-style-position`, and the `list-style` shorthand, inherited.
- `a`: `href` as an absolute `http:`, `https:`, or `mailto:` URL, or `#id`, emitted as one `/Link` annotation per line segment or image. An `http:` or `https:` URL must spell out its host, so `http:foo` rejects. `font-family` lists are read in order and the first entry that is a registered `RenderOptions.fonts` family or a built-in name wins; a registered family beats a built-in of the same name. The built-in names are `Helvetica`, `Arial`, `sans-serif`, `system-ui`, and `ui-sans-serif` for Helvetica, `Times`, `Times New Roman`, `serif`, and `ui-serif` for Times, and `Courier`, `Courier New`, `monospace`, and `ui-monospace` for Courier. Names are case-insensitive, and a quoted `'serif'` is a family name, not the generic keyword. Any other entry, such as `Georgia` or `Inter`, is skipped for the next one, as in a browser. A list where no entry matches rejects with `unregistered font family`, naming the list, so a typo or a forgotten `fonts` entry never silently changes the typeface; end a list with `serif`, `sans-serif`, or `monospace` to always match. Colours accept hex, `rgb()`, `hsl()`, `oklch()`, `oklab()`, `currentcolor`, and CSS named colours; OKLab colours outside sRGB clip per channel, and a colour with alpha rejects; use `opacity` on the element.

- Browser defaults give `p` vertical margins of `1em`, and `h1`-`h6` vertical margins of `0.67em`/`0.83em`/`1em`/`1.33em`/`1.67em`/`2.33em`, measured against each element's font size. `hr` has `0.5em` vertical margins and `th` centres its text. Matching rules and inline styles override these defaults; adjoining vertical margins collapse.
- Flex containers (`display: flex`) lay out one line of items with `flex-direction: row | column`, `gap`, `justify-content` (`flex-start`, `start`, `flex-end`, `end`, `center`, `space-between`, `space-around`, `space-evenly`, `normal`), and `align-items` and `align-self` (`stretch`, `flex-start`, `start`, `flex-end`, `end`, `center`, `normal`, and `auto` for `align-self`). `flex`, `flex-grow`, `flex-shrink`, `flex-basis`, and `align-self` reject outside a flex item rather than being dropped.
- `flex-wrap: nowrap | wrap` on a flex row. A wrapping row fills each row with items until the next item's hypothetical width, its flex basis within `min-width` and `max-width` plus its margins, would pass the row, then starts a new row; every row then grows, shrinks, and justifies its items on its own. `gap` takes one length for both gaps, or the row gap then the column gap; `row-gap` sits between rows and `column-gap` between the items of a row. A row is as tall as its tallest item. `align-content` (`normal`, `stretch`, `flex-start`, `start`, `flex-end`, `end`, `center`, `space-between`, `space-around`, `space-evenly`) shares the space a `height` or `min-height` leaves below the rows; `normal` and `stretch` grow every row by an equal share. Without a height a wrapping row breaks across pages between its rows, and each row moves whole. `flex-wrap: wrap-reverse` rejects, and so does `flex-wrap: wrap` on a flex column. `align-content` on a row that does not wrap changes nothing, as in CSS; `flex-wrap` and `align-content` outside a flex container reject.
- Row items take `flex-grow`, `flex-shrink`, `flex-basis`, and the `flex` shorthand as CSS defines them: `flex: 1` is `1 1 0%`, `auto` is `1 1 auto`, `none` is `0 0 auto`, and a basis alone grows and shrinks by 1. A basis of `auto` starts from the item's `width`, else from its content's longest line; an item shrinks no further than its longest word or image unless `min-width` allows, and `min-width` and `max-width` bound it. `auto` margins on an item take free space first: left and right ones along the row, before `justify-content`, and top and bottom ones across it. A row is as tall as its tallest item, or its `height`; `stretch` grows a painted item without a height to the row, up to its `max-height`. Items wider than the row after shrinking, or taller than its `height`, reject.
- A flex row keeps the column model of earlier releases until the row or an item uses what that model cannot express: `justify-content`, `align-items`, or a height on the row; `align-self`, `min-width`, `max-width`, a height, `box-sizing`, an `auto` margin, or a `flex` value it rejected on an item. In the column model an item without `flex` or `width` takes an equal share, `flex` keeps only its grow factor, and `width` includes the item's margins, padding, and border, so existing documents render unchanged.
- A flex column stacks its items with `gap` and aligns them across with `align-items`, `align-self`, and `auto` side margins; an item that does not stretch and has no `width` shrinks to fit its content. `justify-content`, and `auto` top and bottom margins, share the space a `height` or `min-height` on the column leaves; `justify-content` rejects without one. Column items take no `flex-grow` or `flex-basis`; give them a height. A column that sets none of these stays block flow and splits across pages.
- `width`, `min-width`, `max-width`, `height`, `min-height`, and `max-height` on blocks, paragraphs, headings, `span`, `a`, and `hr`, with `box-sizing: content-box | border-box`. A percent width resolves against the container's width, and a percent height against the parent's fixed `height`; without one it rejects. `margin-left: auto` and `margin-right: auto` centre or push a block narrower than its container, as `mx-auto` with `max-w-*` does; `auto` top and bottom margins are zero in block flow. Content taller than a `height` or `max-height` rejects with `ContentOverflow`, as nothing clips.
- `position: static | relative | absolute | fixed`, `top`, `right`, `bottom`, `left`, the `inset`, `inset-inline`, and `inset-block` shorthands with their `inset-*-start` and `inset-*-end` longhands, and `z-index`, on blocks, paragraphs, headings, `span`, `a`, `img`, and `svg`. An inset is `auto`, a length, or a percent of the containing block, either sign, up to 14,400pt either way; `z-index` is `auto` or a whole number. A relative box keeps its place and size in flow and paints moved by `left`, else minus `right`, and `top`, else minus `bottom`; a percent `left` or `right` is of its container's width, and a percent `top` or `bottom` rejects. An absolute box leaves the flow, so margins collapse past it and its container does not grow for it. Its containing block is the padding box of the nearest relative or absolute ancestor, or the page area, the page inside its `@page` margins, header and footer included, when there is none. It paints on the page where that ancestor starts, or, against the page area, on the page where it sits in flow. A fixed box takes the page area of every page and paints on each, after the header and footer. An absolute or fixed box needs `top` or `bottom`, and `left` or `right`: with both `auto` CSS uses where the box would sit in flow, which the engine does not track, so it rejects. Between `left` and `right` an `auto` width fills, and otherwise shrinks to fit its content within them; between `top` and `bottom` an `auto` height fills, and otherwise follows its content. `left` wins over `right` and `top` over `bottom` when the size is set too. Positioned boxes never split across pages. They paint after all normal-flow content of the page, in `z-index` order and then document order, `auto` as `0`, and those below `0` paint under the page's normal flow. A positioned box with a `z-index` number keeps the positioned boxes inside it in its own layer; under `auto` they join the layer around it.
- `h1`-`h6` defaults of `2em`/`1.5em`/`1.17em`/`1em`/`0.83em`/`0.67em` and bold, which a matching rule or inline style overrides but an inherited `font-size` does not.
- `break-before` and `break-after` as `auto`, `page`, `avoid`, or `avoid-page`; `break-inside` as `auto`, `avoid`, or `avoid-page`; `orphans` and `widows` as whole numbers from 1. See [Page breaks](#page-breaks).
- Tables: `width` and `flex` on each column's first `th`/`td` without `colSpan` size the columns; `colSpan` and `rowSpan`; `padding`, `border`, `background`, `vertical-align: top | middle | bottom`, and text properties on cells; `background` and `vertical-align` on `tr`, and `vertical-align` on row groups; `width`, `margin-top`, `margin-bottom`, `break-inside: avoid`, and `border-collapse: collapse | separate` on `table`; `border-spacing: 0`; `break-inside: avoid` on `tbody` keeps its rows together.

Block children stream independently, including those of boxes, so long `<main>` documents paginate without retaining the whole layout; the renderer holds back at most one page of placed content while a break is undecided. A table's body rows stream unless the table sits in a flex row or a `break-inside: avoid` group, and a streaming table repeats its header and footer rows on every page. Flex rows that do not wrap, each row of a wrapping one, and `break-inside: avoid` groups remain atomic. A single open container, a table's header and footer rows, or one table row may buffer at most 16 MiB of layout commands, and a document may paint at most 16 MiB of content on at most 10,000 pages.

## Intentional v1 limits

- Helvetica, Times, and Courier, each in regular, bold, italic or oblique, and bold italic, are the built-in fonts, and Helvetica is the default. Registered host-provided TTF and OTF (CFF) families support regular and optional bold, italic, and bold italic files, static or [variable](#variable-fonts). `font-feature-settings`, `font-variant-*`, and `text-decoration: overline` reject.
- <a id="cff-fonts"></a>An OTF with CFF outlines embeds as a CIDFontType0 with a `FontFile3` of subtype `CIDFontType0C`. FlashPDF subsets the CFF itself and keeps glyph ids, so an unused glyph becomes an empty charstring and an unused local or global subroutine a bare `return`, which keeps every subroutine number valid. A name-keyed font becomes CID-keyed with an `Adobe-Identity-0` ROS and one Font DICT, and every output has an identity charset, so each glyph id in a show string is its CID. A CID-keyed font keeps its Font DICTs and FDSelect. A glyph that builds an accent with `seac` keeps every subroutine. CFF2, which variable OTFs use, rejects at registration.
- Text in an embedded TTF or OTF renders any character the font has a glyph for, including CJK and supplementary-plane characters. A character the font lacks falls back per cluster, a character with its combining marks, joiners, and variation selectors, to the first registered font that has the whole cluster: faces of the same weight and slant first, in registration order, then the others. Text in a built-in font falls back the same way for a character outside WinAnsi. The fallback font draws with the run's size, colour, and line box. A cluster no registered font has rejects, naming the character and its code point. <a id="text-shaping"></a>HarfRust shapes it one word at a time with the font's default OpenType features, among them `ccmp`, `locl`, `calt`, `liga`, `clig`, `kern`, `mark`, and `mkmk`, so ligatures, contextual alternates, and positioned combining marks render as the font designs them. Letter spacing turns `liga` and `clig` off, as CSS Text asks. The script comes from the word's first letter and the language is unset, so `locl` applies only a font's default forms. A word that crosses inline elements shapes in parts, so no ligature or kerning pair forms across that boundary. Copied text reads back a ligature as all its letters through `/ToUnicode`, and in tagged mode a cluster whose glyphs cannot spell its text, such as one character drawn as two glyphs, carries `/ActualText`. A paragraph with right-to-left text, or with `direction: rtl`, gets [Unicode UAX #9](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr9/) levels around its base direction. Lines break in logical order, and then each line shows its runs in visual order: a right-to-left word shapes right to left, so Arabic joins and brackets mirror, and the words of a right-to-left run read from the right. The bidi controls U+200E, U+200F, U+202A to U+202E, and U+2066 to U+2069 take effect and show no glyph. A word too wide for its column breaks between glyphs in visual order. The built-in faces are WinAnsi standard fonts, so non-Latin text needs an embedded font. They have no embedded program, so PDF/A rejects all twelve. Lines break at ASCII whitespace and at the break opportunities of [Unicode UAX #14](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr14/): after a hyphen or a `/`, between CJK characters, never before closing punctuation such as `!` or `)`. U+00A0 and U+2060 never break, U+200B breaks without a glyph, and U+00AD breaks with a `-` that shows only at the line end. There are no hyphenation dictionaries, and a space always breaks, also before punctuation that UAX #14 keeps on the line.
- Images: 16-bit, 1/2/4-bit, and interlaced PNGs, 12-bit, lossless, hierarchical, and arithmetic-coded JPEGs, and every other format (GIF, WebP) reject naming the feature. A PNG with transparency must decode to at most 64 MiB, and all images in one render are capped at 64 MiB of input. `object-fit` rejects: an image always scales to its box. `min-width`, `min-height`, and a percent `max-height` reject on an image. An image wider than its container or taller than an empty page rejects with the element path; `max-width` and `max-height` bound one that would be. Image bytes are not CRC-checked, and a PNG without transparency passes through without being inflated, so render time stays proportional to the file size; a corrupt file produces a corrupt picture, not an error.
- SVG: text, gradients, patterns, filters, masks, `clipPath`, `use`, `symbol`, `defs`, `image`, `foreignObject`, `<style>` and classes inside a drawing, and any other element, attribute, or style property reject with a reason. `opacity` multiplies into each shape's fill and stroke opacity instead of compositing the group, so overlapping shapes in a translucent `g` show through each other. A drawing holds at most 10,000 elements and 100,000 path segments. A percent `width` together with a `height` rejects unless `preserveAspectRatio="none"`, because the box's aspect ratio is unknown before layout. SVG files may hold XML's five named entities and numeric ones; comments and the XML declaration are skipped, and a DOCTYPE with an internal subset, CDATA, and `class` or `style` on the root reject. Design tools often add editor metadata or a `<style>` block, so run their files through SVGO first.
- Links: `javascript:`, relative, and non-ASCII-after-encoding URLs reject, and so does a `#id` that is empty or not printable ASCII. Link rectangles have no border. A destination always sits at the top of a line, so an empty element's id points at the line after it. Only ids that links name become named destinations, so `#nameddest=` cannot reach an id no link uses.
- A header or footer never splits across pages and cannot hold a page break. With each other they may take at most half the page between its margins. Each `PageNumber` and `TotalPages` counts as five digits when the height is measured, then each page lays out again with its real numbers.
- Gradients, background images, `box-shadow`, `outline`, and `border-image` reject. Children are not clipped to rounded corners. Opacity applies per element, without transparency group compositing.
- Tables have no `colgroup`/`col` or `caption`. `vertical-align: baseline` rejects, since cells with different font sizes or padding would misalign, and `vertical-align` outside table cells and rows takes only the supported inline values. `border-spacing` other than 0 rejects: separate borders tile the row, so adjacent full borders double up; use `border-collapse: collapse` for single rules. With collapsed borders, `hidden` acts as `none` instead of hiding the neighbour's border, and at a page break between two body rows each page shows only its own row's half of that line. A `rowSpan` cell never splits across pages; its rows move whole. `table` takes no padding, border, background, or horizontal margins; style its cells or a wrapping `div`. A table inside a flex row, or a `break-inside: avoid` group is atomic like any other content there.
- `position: sticky` rejects, since a PDF does not scroll. Absolute and fixed boxes reject without a `top` or `bottom` and a `left` or `right`, with `auto` margins, as a flex item, in a header or footer (fixed only), or with `break-before: page` or `break-after: page`. `position` on `table`, `tr`, `td`, `th`, and row groups rejects. A fixed box may not hold a link target or a bookmarked heading, which would repeat on every page. `z-index` orders only positioned boxes: opacity does not start a stacking context, and a fixed box paints over every positioned box of the body whatever their `z-index`.
- `box-decoration-break` rejects: a split box draws its top border and padding on the first page and its bottom ones on the last, as `slice` does.
- Unmatched rules and at-rules other than `@media`, `@property`, and `@page` are skipped silently; `@layer` and `@supports` block contents are skipped too, so import `tailwindcss/utilities` rather than the full preflight. A rule whose selector can match nothing in a PDF is skipped silently as well: a pseudo-element (`::before`, `::after`, `::placeholder`, `::selection`, `::backdrop`), a pseudo-class of user or form state (`:hover`, `:focus`, `:active`, `:visited`, `:focus-visible`, `:disabled`, `:checked`, and the like), `:host`, and a type FlashPDF never renders. Inside `:not()` such a part is simply false, so `:not(:hover)` matches every element, and Tailwind's `hover:` and `focus:` do nothing. Every other selector outside the subset rejects with its reason: attribute selectors (Tailwind's `data-*:` and `aria-*:`), `:has()`, `:lang()`, `:dir()`, `:scope`, `:link`, `:any-link`, `:defined`, `:nth-*(... of <selector>)`, namespaces (`*|a`), unknown pseudo-classes, and malformed selectors. When a selector holds both kinds, such as `a[href]::after`, it can never match and is skipped.
